CragCast
Climbing conditions within driving distance of Seattle

CragCast turns the forecast into a simple, at-a-glance verdict for each crag within driving distance of Seattle. Instead of making you read a wall of temperatures, wind speeds, and precipitation numbers, it answers the only question that matters on a climbing morning: where is the rock dry and worth the drive? Each crag gets a color for each day — green to go, yellow if it’s marginal, red if it’s wet.

FAQ

How are the colors decided?

Green means dry rock and good conditions. Yellow means marginal — recent rain still drying out, too hot for decent friction, or borderline in some other way. Red means wet or unclimbable. The verdict weighs precipitation, how much rain fell recently and how long that rock takes to dry, temperature, wind, and whether the crag sits in sun or shade.

Why is a perfect-temperature day still red?

Wet rock trumps everything. If it rained recently and the rock hasn’t dried — or the crag is one that seeps — it’s red no matter how pleasant the air feels. Climbing on wet rock is dangerous and can break holds, so the forecast errs on the side of caution.

How often does it update?

Once early each morning, so any overnight rain is already factored in before you head out. The “updated” time at the top of the forecast tells you exactly how fresh the data is.

Where does the data come from?

The U.S. National Weather Service. CragCast reads the raw forecast and turns it into the per-crag verdicts you see.

My favorite crag isn’t listed.

The crag list is small and hand-curated for now. If there’s somewhere you’d like to see added, get in touch — I’m always happy to consider it.

Support the crags

CragCast is free, and the best way to support it isn’t to pay me — it’s to take care of the places it sends you. Do trail work when you can. Brush the chalk and tick marks off holds. Pack out your trash, and a little of someone else’s. Respect closures, raptor nesting seasons, and access agreements. Healthy crags and good standing with landowners are what make a tool like this worth having in the first place.
